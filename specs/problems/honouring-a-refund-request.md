# Problem: A payment that should never have happened cannot be undone

**Status:** Draft
**Owner:** Small organizations (the administrator who gets the email) and the
supporter who paid by mistake

## Who has this problem
Every organization collecting supporting members, and any supporter who paid
and should not have: someone who signed up without realising what they were
agreeing to, who meant to support a different organization, who picked the
wrong tier, or who was charged for a renewal they thought they had stopped.

## The problem
The product takes money in seconds and has no way to give it back. The
organization's own sales terms promise the supporter a 14-day right of
withdrawal (*angrerett*) — a legal requirement, not a courtesy — and then send
them off to write an email, because the product offers the administrator
nothing to act on that email with.

So the refund happens somewhere else, in the payment provider's own portal, by
an administrator who has to find their way around a system they otherwise never
use. And when it happens, the product does not notice: the money is back with
the supporter, and the member list still shows them as a paying member, still
counts them, still writes to them. The two records disagree, and the one the
organization actually works from is the wrong one.

## Why it matters
- **It is a promise the product makes and cannot keep.** The terms page is
  generated by us; sending someone to an email address is the product declining
  to solve the problem it created.
- **A wrong member list is worse than a short one.** The whole point of the
  register is that it is true ([keeping an accurate list](keeping-an-accurate-member-list.md)).
  A member who was refunded is a paying member on paper only, and every number
  derived from the list inherits the error.
- **It re-bills people.** Giving the money back does not end the yearly
  arrangement. Unless someone remembers to end it too, the person who asked to
  be let out is charged again next year — the same mistake, on purpose.
- **The volunteer pays the price.** Refunding in a provider portal is exactly
  the kind of unfamiliar, high-stakes admin work this product exists to remove.

## What "solved" looks like
- An administrator can give a supporter their money back **from the back
  office**, without opening the payment provider's portal.
- Giving the money back also **ends the arrangement**, so nobody is charged
  again after being refunded.
- The member list tells the truth immediately afterwards: a refunded period is
  not a supported period.
- The same correction lands even when the refund was made **outside** the
  product — the register follows the money wherever it moved.
